# Gopher Protocol

> [!abstract] Summary
> A TCP/IP application layer protocol designed in the early 1990s for the distribution, search, and retrieval of documents, serving as a functional predecessor to the World Wide Web (p. 3).

## Conceptual Design
The Gopher protocol was designed to organize data through a strict menu-document hierarchy, providing a structured way to navigate information (p. 3). Unlike the open, hyperlink-driven nature of the modern web, Gopher relied on a hierarchical structure that felt more like a folder [[Manual Hierarchical Web Taxonomy|directory]] than a web page.

## Historical Significance
Despite its eventually being surpassed by the World Wide Web, Gopher was a vital part of early internet history. Specialized search tools—most notably Veronica and Jughead—were developed to [[Inverted Index|index]] Gopher-based documents (p. 3). Today, it is regarded as the effective technical and conceptual predecessor to the modern web, setting the stage for subsequent search and retrieval technologies (p. 3).
